# **Product Requirements Document**

## **Citizens Lens**

**Product:** Citizens Lens  
**Category:** Civic education / constitutional information  
**Primary market:** Nigeria  
**Primary users:** Everyday Nigerian citizens  
**Core promise:** Understand your constitutional rights without needing to understand legal language.

---

## **1\. Product Overview**

**Citizens Lens** is a citizen-focused platform that makes the Nigerian Constitution easier to understand and navigate.

The Nigerian Constitution is extensive and written in formal legal language. Citizens Lens addresses the accessibility problem by allowing people to:

* Search the Constitution using ordinary language.  
* Explore constitutional topics and everyday situations.  
* Receive simple explanations of relevant constitutional provisions.  
* Read the original constitutional text.  
* Learn about the Constitution through structured lessons.  
* Find relevant information quickly when dealing with situations involving their rights.

The product should feel less like **reading a legal document** and more like **having a clear guide to understanding the Constitution**.

---

# **2\. Problem**

Many citizens do not know what the Constitution says about their rights, responsibilities, or the powers of public institutions.

The problem is not necessarily that the Constitution is unavailable. It is that **availability does not equal accessibility**.

A citizen may have access to a PDF of the Constitution but still struggle to answer:

> “What does this mean for me?”

Citizens Lens addresses this gap by translating the experience of navigating the Constitution from:

**Legal language → searching through documents → interpreting provisions**

into:

**Everyday question → simple answer → understandable explanation → original source**

---

# **3\. Product Vision**

### **Vision**

**A Nigeria where citizens can easily understand the Constitution and the rights it gives them.**

### **Mission**

To make constitutional information accessible, understandable and useful to ordinary Nigerians.

---

# **4\. Target Users**

### **Primary audience**

**Everyday Nigerian citizens**

This includes people with little or no legal background.

The product should not assume that users:

* know constitutional terminology;  
* know which section they are looking for;  
* understand legal language;  
* know the difference between constitutional provisions and other laws.

### **Secondary audiences**

Citizens Lens can also serve:

* Students  
* Teachers  
* Journalists  
* Researchers  
* Civil society organisations  
* Community leaders  
* Young people  
* Activists  
* Public-interest organisations  
* People studying Nigerian government and civic education

However, these audiences should not make the product unnecessarily complicated for ordinary citizens.

---

# **5\. Core User Experience**

Citizens Lens should have **three primary ways of using the product**.

### **1\. Search**

The primary entry point.

Users should be able to ask questions in ordinary language.

Examples:

> “What are my rights if I'm arrested?”

> “Can I peacefully protest?”

> “What does the Constitution say about freedom of expression?”

> “What are my rights as a Nigerian citizen?”

The user should not need to know the relevant constitutional section.

---

### **2\. Explore by Situation**

Users who don't know exactly what to search for can browse situations.

Possible categories include:

* Arrest & detention  
* Personal liberty  
* Freedom of expression  
* Peaceful assembly  
* Privacy  
* Property  
* Fair hearing  
* Citizenship  
* Elections & political participation  
* Government powers  
* Courts & justice  
* Duties of citizens

The categories should eventually be based on actual user behaviour rather than assumptions.

---

### **3\. Learn**

Two learning paths should exist.

#### **Understand the Constitution**

A structured beginner journey covering concepts such as:

* What a constitution is  
* How Nigeria's Constitution works  
* Fundamental rights  
* Government institutions  
* Federal and state powers  
* Citizenship  
* Elections and political participation  
* Courts and justice  
* Constitutional protections

#### **Learn Through Real-Life Situations**

Scenario-based learning.

For example:

> **You're arrested. What does the Constitution say about your rights?**

> **You want to participate in a peaceful demonstration. What constitutional issues should you understand?**

> **You have a dispute with a public authority. What constitutional protections might be relevant?**

This allows users to learn constitutional concepts through situations they can recognise.

---

# **6\. Search Experience**

Search should be the **heart of Citizens Lens**.

The user enters a question.

For example:

> **Can the police arrest me without telling me why?**

Citizens Lens should return a **direct answer first**.

### **Example structure**

**Short answer**

A concise explanation written in ordinary language.

Then:

### **What the Constitution says**

The relevant constitutional provision.

### **Read more**

A deeper explanation for users who want more context.

### **Original provision**

The actual constitutional text.

### **Related topics**

Links to other relevant constitutional subjects.

The product should avoid presenting a long wall of legal text immediately after a search.

---

# **7\. Plain-Language Explanations**

This is one of the most important parts of Citizens Lens.

The product should distinguish between:

**The Constitution**

and

**Citizens Lens' explanation of the Constitution.**

The original text should remain identifiable as the authoritative source.

The plain-language explanation should:

* Avoid unnecessary legal jargon.  
* Use everyday language.  
* Explain complicated concepts.  
* Preserve important qualifications.  
* Avoid changing the meaning of the original provision.  
* Avoid presenting an absolute right where the Constitution contains limitations or exceptions.

This is particularly important because simplifying legal language can accidentally **oversimplify the law**.

---

# **8\. “Read More”**

The product should not force every user to read a lengthy explanation.

A user should be able to stop after the short answer or continue.

### **Progressive information structure**

**Short answer**

↓

**Read more**

↓

**Detailed explanation**

↓

**Original constitutional provision**

↓

**Related information**

This allows Citizens Lens to work for both:

* the person who needs a quick understanding; and  
* the person who wants to study the issue.

---

# **9\. “I Need Help Now”**

This should exist, but **not dominate the product**.

It can sit beneath Search or within the Explore section.

Possible situations:

* I've been arrested  
* I'm being detained  
* I believe my rights have been violated  
* I'm dealing with a government official  
* I need to understand a government decision  
* I want to understand my rights in a dispute

The experience should provide **relevant constitutional information**, not pretend to replace a lawyer, police officer, court, or emergency service.

For potentially urgent situations, the product should clearly distinguish:

> **Information about your constitutional rights**

from:

> **Legal advice or professional assistance.**

---

# **10\. Constitution Library**

Users should also be able to browse the Constitution directly.

Possible navigation:

**Constitution**

→ Parts  
→ Chapters  
→ Sections  
→ Topics

But this should be a secondary experience rather than the main experience.

The product's philosophy should be:

> **You don't need to know where the answer is. Citizens Lens helps you find it.**

---

# **11\. Topic Pages**

Every major constitutional topic can have its own page.

For example:

### **Fundamental Rights**

The page could contain:

* What are fundamental rights?  
* Rights covered  
* Simple explanations  
* Relevant constitutional provisions  
* Related situations  
* Common questions  
* Learn more

This creates a bridge between search and structured learning.

---

# **12\. Common Questions**

Citizens Lens should identify questions ordinary citizens frequently ask.

For example:

**“Can I be arrested without a warrant?”**

**“What happens when someone is detained?”**

**“What does freedom of expression mean?”**

**“What does the Constitution say about peaceful assembly?”**

This can become one of the product's most useful discovery mechanisms.

---

# **13\. Myth vs Constitution**

I strongly recommend this feature.

Users could see common claims such as:

> **“The Constitution says everyone has an absolute right to do X.”**

Citizens Lens then explains what the Constitution actually provides, including relevant qualifications.

The goal isn't to shame people for believing something incorrect.

The goal is:

**Claim → What the Constitution actually says → Explanation → Source**

This could also make Citizens Lens highly shareable.

---

# **14\. Constitutional Glossary**

A simple glossary should explain legal and constitutional terminology.

For example:

**Fundamental right**

**Personal liberty**

**Fair hearing**

**Legislative power**

**Executive power**

**Judicial power**

**Federalism**

etc.

The glossary should use simple explanations rather than reproducing dictionary-style legal definitions.

---

# **15\. Save & Return**

Users should be able to save:

* Constitutional provisions  
* Questions  
* Topics  
* Learning materials

This turns Citizens Lens from something people visit once into something they can return to.

---

# **16\. Share**

A user should be able to share a specific piece of constitutional information.

For example:

> **Your constitutional rights regarding personal liberty**

rather than simply sending someone an entire Constitution PDF.

This makes the product useful in conversations, classrooms, communities and social media.

---

# **17\. Learning Progress**

For the learning section, users should be able to see their progress.

For example:

**Understanding the Constitution**

4 of 10 lessons completed.

This should remain lightweight rather than turning Citizens Lens into a complicated education platform.

---

# **18\. “What Changed?”**

This is a feature I would add because of the nature of the product.

Citizens Lens should distinguish between:

### **Current Constitution**

What is currently in force.

### **Proposed Changes**

Bills or proposals seeking to alter the Constitution.

### **Previous Versions/Changes**

Relevant historical changes.

This distinction is essential. The National Assembly's records show that constitutional alteration bills continue to be introduced, meaning Citizens Lens cannot treat every proposed amendment as though it is already part of the Constitution. ([NASS](https://nass.gov.ng/documents/download/11132?utm_source=chatgpt.com))

---

# **19\. Source Transparency**

Every explanation should allow users to see:

**Source**

→ Constitutional section  
→ Relevant document/version  
→ Date/version information where appropriate

The principle should be:

> **“Don't just tell me. Show me where it comes from.”**

This is particularly important for a product dealing with constitutional rights.

---

# **20\. Legal Safety & Product Boundaries**

This is one of the biggest loopholes in the concept.

Citizens Lens can easily drift from:

**“Here's what the Constitution says.”**

into:

**“Here's what you should do legally.”**

Those are not the same thing.

Therefore, the product should establish a clear boundary.

### **Citizens Lens should provide:**

* Constitutional information  
* Plain-language explanations  
* Educational content  
* Sources  
* Context  
* Related legal information where appropriately sourced

### **Citizens Lens should be careful about:**

* Giving personalised legal advice  
* Telling someone they will win a legal case  
* Giving definitive answers where other laws affect the situation  
* Treating constitutional rights as absolute where limitations exist  
* Telling users to confront authorities based solely on information in the app  
* Presenting an interpretation as though it were the constitutional text

---

# **21\. Another Major Loophole: Constitution ≠ Entire Nigerian Law**

This is probably the **most important conceptual issue** I'd flag.

A user might ask:

> “Can the police do this?”

The Constitution may be relevant, but it may not contain the entire answer.

Other legislation, regulations and judicial decisions can matter.

Therefore, Citizens Lens should eventually have a distinction such as:

### **Constitution**

What the Constitution says.

### **Other Law**

Other relevant legal rules.

### **Court Interpretation**

How courts have interpreted relevant provisions, where appropriate.

That could eventually make Citizens Lens much more valuable without changing its core purpose.

---

# **22\. Another Loophole: Interpretation**

Two people can read the same constitutional provision and disagree about what it means in a particular circumstance.

Citizens Lens should therefore avoid presenting every explanation as though it is an unquestionable legal conclusion.

Where interpretation is genuinely contested, the product should be able to communicate:

> **The constitutional text says X. Courts/scholars have interpreted this in different ways.**

The product informs the citizen rather than pretending to be the final authority.

---

# **23\. Another Loophole: Outdated Information**

This product will lose trust very quickly if a provision changes and the app continues displaying old information as current.

Therefore, **content review and update processes are core product requirements**, not an afterthought.

The user should be able to understand whether they are reading:

* Current constitutional text  
* Historical text  
* Proposed amendment  
* Educational explanation  
* Other legal material

---

# **24\. Another Loophole: “Plain English” Can Become Inaccurate English**

This is subtle.

The easier you make the explanation, the greater the temptation to remove qualifications.

For example:

> **Legal text:** complicated but precise.

becomes:

> **Plain explanation:** simple but potentially misleading.

Citizens Lens should therefore optimise for:

**Simple \+ accurate**

rather than:

**Simple at all costs.**

---

# **25\. Trust Principles**

I would make these product principles:

### **1\. Source first**

Show users where information comes from.

### **2\. Plain language**

Explain without unnecessary legal jargon.

### **3\. No hidden interpretation**

Make it clear when Citizens Lens is explaining rather than quoting.

### **4\. Context matters**

Don't strip away important exceptions or qualifications.

### **5\. Current information**

Clearly distinguish current law from proposed or historical changes.

### **6\. Citizen-first**

The product should assume the user is intelligent but may not understand legal language.

### **7\. Neutrality**

Citizens Lens should educate citizens about their rights and government without becoming a political campaign or advocacy platform.

---

# **26\. Suggested Navigation**

I'd keep the main navigation extremely simple:

**Home**

**Explore**

**Learn**

**Saved**

### **Home**

Search bar \+ situations \+ popular questions.

### **Explore**

Browse constitutional topics.

### **Learn**

Structured constitutional education \+ real-life scenarios.

### **Saved**

Saved questions, provisions and learning materials.

The Constitution itself can be accessible throughout the product rather than necessarily being a separate primary navigation item.

---

Build trust from day one.

# **29\. Success Metrics**

The most important question isn't simply:

> “How many people downloaded Citizens Lens?”

I'd measure whether people are actually **understanding the Constitution**.

Important measures include:

* Number of questions searched  
* Most common questions  
* Search-to-answer completion  
* “Read more” engagement  
* Provisions viewed  
* Learning completion  
* Saved provisions  
* Shared content  
* Repeat usage  
* Users returning to previously viewed topics  
* User-reported understanding  
* Searches that produce no useful result

That last one is particularly valuable.

If 10,000 people search for questions Citizens Lens cannot answer well, you've discovered what the product needs to improve.

> **Legal text:** complicated but precise.

becomes:

> **Plain explanation:** simple but potentially misleading.

Citizens Lens should therefore optimise for:

**Simple \+ accurate**

rather than:

**Simple at all costs.**

---

# **25\. Trust Principles**

I would make these product principles:

### **1\. Source first**

Show users where information comes from.

### **2\. Plain language**

Explain without unnecessary legal jargon.

### **3\. No hidden interpretation**

Make it clear when Citizens Lens is explaining rather than quoting.

### **4\. Context matters**

Don't strip away important exceptions or qualifications.

### **5\. Current information**

Clearly distinguish current law from proposed or historical changes.

### **6\. Citizen-first**

The product should assume the user is intelligent but may not understand legal language.

### **7\. Neutrality**

Citizens Lens should educate citizens about their rights and government without becoming a political campaign or advocacy platform.

# **29\. Success Metrics**

The most important question isn't simply:

> “How many people downloaded Citizens Lens?”

I'd measure whether people are actually **understanding the Constitution**.

Important measures include:

* Number of questions searched  
* Most common questions  
* Search-to-answer completion  
* “Read more” engagement  
* Provisions viewed  
* Learning completion  
* Saved provisions  
* Shared content  
* Repeat usage  
* Users returning to previously viewed topics  
* User-reported understanding  
* Searches that produce no useful result

That last one is particularly valuable.

If 10,000 people search for questions Citizens Lens cannot answer well, you've discovered what the product needs to improve.

## **The product positioning I'd use**

> **Citizens Lens**

> **See your rights clearly.**

> Understand the Nigerian Constitution in plain language, find the provisions that matter to you, and learn how the Constitution works.

One final observation: **the strongest version of Citizens Lens isn't “an app that explains the Constitution.” It's a citizen interface to the Constitution.** The Constitution remains the source; Citizens Lens makes that source understandable, searchable and usable.

And I would keep that distinction at the centre of the product as you develop it.
